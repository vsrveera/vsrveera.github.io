24  From Scores to Probabilities

NoteKey ideas
  • A network outputs logits: unconstrained real numbers. Sigmoid and softmax turn them into probabilities.
  • Softmax is shift-invariant and has a clean temperature knob: dividing logits by \(T\) sharpens or flattens the distribution.
  • Softmax must be computed with the log-sum-exp trick, or it overflows.
  • The gradient of cross-entropy with respect to the logits is \(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{y}\).
  • A model can be accurate and miscalibrated. Temperature scaling repairs the probabilities without changing the ranking.

24.1 The system problem

The last layer of a network produces numbers such as \((2.3,-0.7,4.1)\). These can be negative, they do not sum to 1, and they are not probabilities. Yet we compute losses on probabilities (Chapter 15), sample from them (Chapter 33), and report them to users.

A network outputs unconstrained numbers. How do we turn them into valid, trustworthy probabilities?

24.2 Sigmoid: two classes

For one logit \(z\), the sigmoid gives the probability of the positive class:

\[ \sigma(z)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-z}}\in(0,1). \tag{24.1}\]

The inverse is the logit (log-odds): \(z=\ln\frac{p}{1-p}\). A logit of 0 means 50%. A logit of 2 means 88%. A logit of 5 means 99.3%.

24.3 Softmax: many classes

For \(K\) logits \(\mathbf{z}\), the softmax is

\[ p_i=\text{softmax}(\mathbf{z})_i=\frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_{j=1}^{K}e^{z_j}}. \tag{24.2}\]

It is positive, sums to 1, and keeps the order of the logits. Exponentiating makes a gap in logits into a ratio of probabilities: \(p_i/p_j=e^{z_i-z_j}\).

Properties:

  1. Shift invariance. \(\text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}+c\mathbf{1})=\text{softmax}(\mathbf{z})\). Only differences of logits matter.
  2. Two classes reduce to the sigmoid. \(\text{softmax}(z_1,z_2)_1=\sigma(z_1-z_2)\).
  3. Monotone. A larger logit gives a larger probability.

24.3.1 Temperature

Dividing the logits by a temperature \(T>0\) changes the sharpness:

\[ p_i^{(T)}=\frac{e^{z_i/T}}{\sum_je^{z_j/T}}. \tag{24.3}\]

As \(T\to0\) the distribution approaches a one-hot vector at the largest logit (argmax). As \(T\to\infty\) it approaches uniform. At \(T=1\) it is the ordinary softmax.

def softmax(z, T=1.0):
    z = np.asarray(z, float) / T
    z = z - z.max(axis=-1, keepdims=True)           # stable: subtract the maximum
    e = np.exp(z)
    return e / e.sum(axis=-1, keepdims=True)

def entropy_bits(p):
    p = p[p > 0]; return -np.sum(p * np.log2(p))

logits = np.array([3.0, 2.0, 1.0, 0.0, -1.0])
fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 4, figsize=(11, 2.6), sharey=True)
for ax, T in zip(axes, [0.25, 1.0, 3.0, 10.0]):
    p = softmax(logits, T)
    ax.bar(range(5), p, color=PALETTE["model"])
    ax.set_title(f"T = {T}\nentropy {entropy_bits(p):.2f} bits", fontsize=9)
axes[0].set_ylabel("probability")
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()

24.4 Computing softmax safely

The exponential overflows for large logits. In 32-bit floats, \(e^{89}\) is already infinity. Because softmax is shift-invariant, we subtract the maximum first. Then the largest exponent is 0, and nothing overflows.

For the loss we need \(\ln p_i=z_i-\ln\sum_je^{z_j}\). The second term is the log-sum-exp:

\[ \text{LSE}(\mathbf{z})=m+\ln\sum_je^{z_j-m},\qquad m=\max_jz_j. \tag{24.4}\]

z = np.array([1000.0, 1001.0, 1002.0])
with np.errstate(over="ignore", invalid="ignore"):
    naive = np.exp(z) / np.exp(z).sum()
print("naive softmax :", naive)
print("stable softmax:", np.round(softmax(z), 4))

def log_softmax(z):
    m = z.max(axis=-1, keepdims=True)
    return z - (m + np.log(np.exp(z - m).sum(axis=-1, keepdims=True)))
print("log-softmax   :", np.round(log_softmax(z), 4))
naive softmax : [nan nan nan]
stable softmax: [0.09   0.2447 0.6652]
log-softmax   : [-2.4076 -1.4076 -0.4076]

Never compute log(softmax(z)). Use log_softmax. Libraries fuse the log-softmax with the loss (cross_entropy takes logits) for this reason.

24.5 The Jacobian of softmax

For backpropagation we need \(\partial p_i/\partial z_j\).

TipDerivation

Write \(p_i=e^{z_i}/S\) with \(S=\sum_ke^{z_k}\). Then \(\partial S/\partial z_j=e^{z_j}\).

  • If \(i=j\): \(\dfrac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_i}=\dfrac{e^{z_i}S-e^{z_i}e^{z_i}}{S^2}=p_i(1-p_i)\).
  • If \(i\ne j\): \(\dfrac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j}=\dfrac{-e^{z_i}e^{z_j}}{S^2}=-p_ip_j\).

Together, \[ \frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j}=p_i(\delta_{ij}-p_j),\qquad\mathbf{J}=\text{diag}(\mathbf{p})-\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}^{\intercal}.\ \blacksquare \]

The rows of \(\mathbf{J}\) sum to zero (the probabilities always sum to 1). Where one \(p_i\) is near 1, every entry of \(\mathbf{J}\) is near 0: softmax saturates, as the sigmoid does (Chapter 10).

24.5.1 Cross-entropy on logits

The loss for true class \(c\) is \(\ell=-\ln p_c\). Its gradient with respect to the logits follows from the Jacobian:

\[ \frac{\partial\ell}{\partial z_j}=-\frac{1}{p_c}\,\frac{\partial p_c}{\partial z_j}=-\frac{1}{p_c}\,p_c(\delta_{cj}-p_j)=p_j-\delta_{cj}. \]

In vector form,

\[ \nabla_{\mathbf{z}}\ell=\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{y}, \tag{24.5}\]

with \(\mathbf{y}\) the one-hot label. The gradient is the prediction minus the target. We verify the Jacobian and this gradient numerically.

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
z = rng.normal(size=5); c = 2
p = softmax(z)
J = np.diag(p) - np.outer(p, p)
eps = 1e-6
J_num = np.stack([(softmax(z + eps * e) - softmax(z - eps * e)) / (2 * eps)
                  for e in np.eye(5)], axis=1)
print("max |analytic − numeric| Jacobian:", np.abs(J - J_num).max())
print("rows sum to zero:", np.allclose(J.sum(axis=1), 0))

loss = lambda z: -np.log(softmax(z)[c])
g_num = np.array([(loss(z + eps * e) - loss(z - eps * e)) / (2 * eps) for e in np.eye(5)])
y = np.eye(5)[c]
print("max |(p − y) − numeric gradient|:", np.abs((p - y) - g_num).max())
max |analytic − numeric| Jacobian: 2.923021547029947e-11
rows sum to zero: True
max |(p − y) − numeric gradient|: 9.871489736745787e-11

24.6 The probability simplex

Three-class probabilities live on a triangle, the simplex: all \((p_1,p_2,p_3)\) with \(p_{i}\ge0\) and \(\sum p_i=1\). Corners are certain outcomes. The centre is the uniform distribution. Softmax maps all of \(\mathbb{R}^3\) onto the interior.

corners = np.array([[0.0, 0.0], [1.0, 0.0], [0.5, np.sqrt(3) / 2]])
to_xy = lambda P: P @ corners
g = np.linspace(0, 1, 120)
U, V = np.meshgrid(g, g)
mask = U + V <= 1
P = np.stack([U[mask], V[mask], 1 - U[mask] - V[mask]], axis=1)
XY = to_xy(P)

fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(9, 3.9))
sc = axes[0].scatter(*XY.T, c=P.max(axis=1), s=4, cmap="viridis")
axes[0].set_title("largest probability"); plt.colorbar(sc, ax=axes[0], fraction=0.04)
zz = np.stack(np.meshgrid(np.linspace(-3, 3, 13), np.linspace(-3, 3, 13)), -1).reshape(-1, 2)
zz = np.c_[zz, np.zeros(len(zz))]
for T, col in [(1.0, PALETTE["data"]), (0.3, PALETTE["loss"])]:
    axes[1].scatter(*to_xy(softmax(zz, T)).T, s=10, color=col, label=f"T = {T}")
axes[1].legend(fontsize=8); axes[1].set_title("softmax of a logit grid")
for ax in axes:
    t = np.vstack([corners, corners[:1]]); ax.plot(*t.T, color="black", lw=1)
    ax.set_aspect("equal"); ax.axis("off")
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()

24.7 Calibration

A model is calibrated if, among predictions with confidence 80%, about 80% are correct. Softmax outputs are not guaranteed to be calibrated. Deep networks trained to low cross-entropy on their training data are often over-confident on new data.

We measure it with a reliability diagram: bin predictions by confidence and plot the accuracy in each bin. A calibrated model lies on the diagonal. The expected calibration error (ECE) is the weighted average gap:

\[ \text{ECE}=\sum_{b}\frac{n_b}{N}\,\bigl|\text{acc}(b)-\text{conf}(b)\bigr|. \tag{24.6}\]

We build an over-confident model on purpose: a flexible network trained on noisy labels for many steps.

import torch, torch.nn as nn
from sklearn.datasets import make_classification
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split

X, y = make_classification(n_samples=3000, n_features=10, n_informative=4, n_classes=3,
                           n_clusters_per_class=2, flip_y=0.15, class_sep=1.0, random_state=0)
Xtr, Xrest, ytr, yrest = train_test_split(X, y, test_size=0.5, random_state=0)
Xval, Xte, yval, yte = train_test_split(Xrest, yrest, test_size=0.6, random_state=0)
T_ = lambda a: torch.tensor(a, dtype=torch.float32)
Xtr_t, Xval_t, Xte_t = T_(Xtr), T_(Xval), T_(Xte)
ytr_t = torch.tensor(ytr)

torch.manual_seed(0)
net = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(10, 128), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(128, 128), nn.ReLU(),
                    nn.Linear(128, 3))
opt = torch.optim.Adam(net.parameters(), lr=0.01)
for _ in range(600):
    opt.zero_grad(); nn.functional.cross_entropy(net(Xtr_t), ytr_t).backward(); opt.step()

with torch.no_grad():
    logits_val, logits_te = net(Xval_t).numpy(), net(Xte_t).numpy()

def ece(probs, labels, bins=10):
    conf = probs.max(1); pred = probs.argmax(1); correct = (pred == labels)
    edges = np.linspace(0.0, 1.0, bins + 1); total = 0.0; rows = []
    for lo, hi in zip(edges[:-1], edges[1:]):
        m = (conf > lo) & (conf <= hi)
        if m.sum():
            rows.append((conf[m].mean(), correct[m].mean(), m.sum()))
            total += m.mean() * abs(correct[m].mean() - conf[m].mean())
    return total, rows

p_te = softmax(logits_te)
e_before, rows_before = ece(p_te, yte)
print(f"test accuracy {np.mean(p_te.argmax(1) == yte):.3f}   mean confidence {p_te.max(1).mean():.3f}"
      f"   ECE {e_before:.3f}")
test accuracy 0.660   mean confidence 0.957   ECE 0.297

The model’s average confidence is higher than its accuracy. This is over-confidence.

24.7.1 Temperature scaling

The simplest repair is to divide all logits by one number \(T\) found on a held-out validation set by minimising the cross-entropy:

\[ T^*=\arg\min_T\;-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\ln\text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_i/T)_{y_i}. \tag{24.7}\]

Since \(T\) rescales all logits equally, the argmax is unchanged. Accuracy stays the same. Only the confidence changes.

from scipy.optimize import minimize_scalar
nll = lambda T: -np.mean(log_softmax(logits_val / T)[np.arange(len(yval)), yval])
T_star = minimize_scalar(nll, bounds=(0.2, 200), method="bounded").x
p_te_T = softmax(logits_te, T_star)
e_after, rows_after = ece(p_te_T, yte)
print(f"fitted temperature T* = {T_star:.2f}")
print(f"ECE before {e_before:.3f}  ->  after {e_after:.3f}")
print(f"accuracy before {np.mean(p_te.argmax(1) == yte):.3f}, "
      f"after {np.mean(p_te_T.argmax(1) == yte):.3f}")

fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(9, 3.8))
for ax, rows, title in zip(axes, [rows_before, rows_after],
                           [f"raw softmax (ECE {e_before:.3f})", f"T = {T_star:.2f} (ECE {e_after:.3f})"]):
    conf = np.array([r[0] for r in rows]); acc = np.array([r[1] for r in rows])
    ax.bar(conf, acc, width=0.07, color=PALETTE["model"], edgecolor="white")
    ax.plot([0, 1], [0, 1], "--", color="black"); ax.set_title(title)
    ax.set_xlabel("confidence"); ax.set_ylabel("accuracy"); ax.set_xlim(0.3, 1.02); ax.set_ylim(0, 1.05)
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()
fitted temperature T* = 10.09
ECE before 0.297  ->  after 0.057
accuracy before 0.660, after 0.660

24.8 Label smoothing

A related idea acts at training time. Instead of a one-hot target, use \(y_k=1-\varepsilon\) for the true class and \(\varepsilon/(K-1)\) for the others. This discourages logits from growing without limit, since a one-hot target is reached only by an infinite logit gap. Smoothing usually improves calibration and is common in classification and translation models.

24.9 Production

PyTorch fuses log-softmax and cross-entropy. We check that our code matches.

import torch.nn.functional as F
logits_t = torch.tensor(rng.normal(size=(6, 5)) * 4)
labels_t = torch.tensor([0, 3, 1, 4, 2, 0])

print("softmax        :", torch.allclose(F.softmax(logits_t, 1), torch.tensor(softmax(logits_t.numpy()))))
print("log_softmax    :", torch.allclose(F.log_softmax(logits_t, 1), torch.tensor(log_softmax(logits_t.numpy()))))
ours = -log_softmax(logits_t.numpy())[np.arange(6), labels_t.numpy()].mean()
print("cross_entropy  :", np.isclose(F.cross_entropy(logits_t, labels_t).item(), ours))

eps_ls, K = 0.1, 5
target = np.full((6, K), eps_ls / (K - 1)); target[np.arange(6), labels_t.numpy()] = 1 - eps_ls
ours_ls = -(target * log_softmax(logits_t.numpy())).sum(1).mean()
print("label smoothing:", np.isclose(F.cross_entropy(logits_t, labels_t, label_smoothing=eps_ls * K / (K - 1)).item(), ours_ls))
softmax        : True
log_softmax    : True
cross_entropy  : True
label smoothing: True

24.10 Failure modes

Overflow. exp of a large logit is infinite. Always subtract the maximum, or use library functions.

Saturation. If one class has a very large logit, \(\mathbf{J}\approx\mathbf{0}\) and the gradient vanishes. It is harmless when the prediction is right and harmful when it is confidently wrong (the cross-entropy gradient \(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{y}\) remains large in that case, which is why we pair softmax with cross-entropy).

Over-confidence. Low training loss does not give calibrated probabilities. Always check a reliability diagram before using probabilities in decisions.

Distribution shift. Calibration fitted on one distribution may not hold on another (Chapter 27).

Ties in argmax. Equal logits make the argmax arbitrary. In float16, many logits may be equal after rounding.

Wrong activation for the task. Softmax for multi-label tasks (several classes can be true at once) is wrong. Use an independent sigmoid per class.

Temperature misuse. A temperature that is fitted for calibration (a post-hoc scale) is not the same as sampling temperature in generation, though the formula is identical (Chapter 33).

24.12 System connection

NoteWhat the system gained in this chapter

The system can now express its output as a probability distribution: logits become probabilities through sigmoid or softmax, with a temperature control, a stable computation, a clean gradient (\(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{y}\)), and a way to check and repair calibration.

The loss function, cross-entropy, was assumed in Chapters 14 and 15. Chapter 25 explains where it comes from: it is what maximum likelihood estimation gives.

Chapter summary

  • Sigmoid: \(\sigma(z)=1/(1+e^{-z})\). Softmax: \(p_i=e^{z_i}/\sum_je^{z_j}\). Two-class softmax is a sigmoid of the logit difference.
  • Softmax is shift-invariant. Temperature \(T\) rescales the logits.
  • Compute with the max-subtraction and log-sum-exp trick.
  • \(\partial p_i/\partial z_j=p_i(\delta_{ij}-p_j)\). The cross-entropy gradient on logits is \(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{y}\).
  • Calibration: confidence should match accuracy. Measure with reliability diagrams and ECE.
  • Temperature scaling fixes confidence without changing predictions.
